Overview

Context

Autonomous navigation requires a careful combination of signal processing, perception, and motion control. This project explored how those layers can come together in a compact experimental platform to reason about environment awareness and safe motion decisions.

Challenge

The problem

The challenge was to reduce complexity into a functional autonomous driving stack: environment sensing, route understanding, and actuation logic that can move a vehicle in a controlled, repeatable way.

Project Snapshot

  • Domain: Autonomous vehicles
  • Focus: Perception and motion control
  • Impact: System-level prototyping
Journey

What was built

The system combined sensor-driven understanding with practical control strategies to emulate autonomous behavior in a constrained environment. The emphasis was not only on technical feasibility but also on reducing the decision-making pipeline into something testable, understandable, and iterative.

Architecture

System thinking

Perception

Sensor integration and environment reading formed the foundation for autonomous decision-making.

Control

Applied motion logic translated perception into safe and deliberate vehicle behavior.

Impact

Results and lessons

This project reinforced how autonomous systems are as much about system architecture and disciplined design as they are about raw hardware or algorithms. It became a practical study in making complex behavior manageable, reliable, and explainable.
