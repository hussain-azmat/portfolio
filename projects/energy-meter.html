Overview

Context

Electricity consumption is often invisible to the end user until the bill arrives. This project explored a practical way to improve visibility by designing a smart energy monitoring system that could quantify usage in real time and expose the data through a web-connected interface. The result was a system intended to reduce energy waste, improve accountability, and support more transparent utility interaction.

Challenge

The problem

The challenge was to design a compact and affordable energy sensing platform that could measure voltage and current accurately, convert that data into usable power and energy values, and communicate the result wirelessly. This had to be done while keeping the board practical to fabricate and robust enough for real household or appliance-level monitoring.

Project Snapshot

  • Industry: Smart energy
  • Focus: Real-time monitoring and wireless reporting
  • Impact: Better consumer awareness and billing transparency
Journey

What was built

The design journey started with the ADE7757 energy metering IC, selected for its precision and suitability for single-phase energy measurement. From there, the work progressed into PCB design, component selection, signal conditioning, calibration, and firmware development for power and energy computation. The final system was intended to present values to the user through an LCD display and a web-based interface connected through an ESP8266 module.

Architecture

System thinking

Hardware

Energy measurement was handled by a dedicated metering IC, with support circuitry for sensing current and voltage, signal integrity, PCB routing, and 3D-printed enclosure integration.

Software

Firmware converted pulse data into power and energy readings, while the connected interface exposed live values for monitoring and analysis over a local or web-based dashboard.

Impact

Results and lessons

The project showed how embedded measurement systems can make energy usage much more tangible. Beyond the technical work, it reinforced a broader lesson: the most useful smart systems are not just connected — they are understandable. When users can see how power is consumed, they are better positioned to act on it, whether that means improving efficiency, reducing waste, or solving billing disputes.
